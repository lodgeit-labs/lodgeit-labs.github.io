Open challenge

The Division 36 challenge

Opened 12 November 2026. Open to anyone, in particular teams building AI for Australian tax.

We have written Division 36 of the ITAA 1997, the ordering of prior-year tax losses against net exempt income and taxable income, as rules a practitioner can read and a machine can run. We think they are right. We would like you to show us where they are not.

What you are challenging

  • File: div36_rev3.le (revision 3, built 9 November 2026)
  • SHA-256: ace4a35200ed0887471e87cccf5b37c4ea9de6e36c985a2ba36b9b6681da5dc7
  • Tested on: LogicalEnglish2 commit a4c314b, SWI-Prolog 9.0.4
  • Contents: 61 rule clauses, each beside the subsection it states; 48 scenarios; 321 tests, all passing
  • Status: revision 3, under review, not yet accepted. No practitioner has signed this file. It is the third revision; five reviewers said “fix” on the first.

The files are below: div36_rev3.le, the test run, the findings log for this revision, and a manifest of fingerprints.

Scope. One Australian-resident individual, resident for the full year. Sections 36-10, 36-15 and 36-20, with sections 26-55 and 4-15. One loss amount per loss year. Not a partnership. The file’s header lists every assumption. Outside that scope the rules refuse, and say why.

Two ways to joust

1. Break ours

Find facts inside the stated scope where the file gives a wrong figure, or gives a figure when it should ask for a missing fact or refuse.

Send us:

  1. the facts, in the terms the file uses;
  2. the figure the file gives;
  3. the figure you say is right;
  4. your derivation from the Act, subsection by subsection.

We run your facts on the published fingerprint. If we reproduce the problem, the case is accepted: we publish it with your name (or initials, or no name, as you choose), add it to the tests, and fix the file as a new revision.

2. Match ours

Run our published scenarios through your own system and send us what it says. For a result to be a fair comparison:

  • Use the facts as written. Do not add, drop or reword any.
  • Answer every scenario, including the ones that should not produce a figure. Some scenarios are missing a fact: the right answer names it. Some are out of scope: the right answer is a refusal. A plausible figure is a fail on both.
  • Run each scenario twice and tell us whether the two answers were identical.
  • Show the working: the provisions your system applied, in order.
  • Say what you ran: the system, its version and the date.
  • Do not tune on the expected answers. They are published, so this is on your honour. If you want your result listed as verified, we will send a small set of unpublished scenarios and ask for answers within a day.

We publish your result only with your consent, and exactly as you sent it.

What is not a challenge

  • “That looks wrong”, without a derivation.
  • A different figure with no provision behind it.
  • A case outside the stated scope. Tell us if you think the scope should be wider; that is useful, but it is a request, not a defect.
  • A difference from an ATO worksheet alone. The Act is the reference. We have already raised one worksheet gap with the ATO (a loss year in which net exempt income exceeds that year’s excess of deductions) and are waiting for a reply.

What we commit to

  • Every accepted case is published, with the fix.
  • A challenge we cannot reproduce is returned with the reason.
  • We will not describe your system publicly without your consent.
  • If you break the file, we will say so here.

How to enter

Email support@lodgeit.net.au with the subject Division 36 challenge. Attach your cases or results as plain text or Markdown.
