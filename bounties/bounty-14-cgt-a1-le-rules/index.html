This is a rule-ratification bounty

Like Bounty #11 (Division 36) and Bounty #12 (its revision), you won’t be checking a calculator. You’ll be checking the law itself, written out as rules — and telling us whether we’ve stated it correctly.

The language is Logical English — developed by Bob Kowalski at Imperial, and LodgeiT is a co-owner of the current version. It produces a proof tree in plain English for every answer, and it refuses to answer when it hasn’t been told enough. But a rule is only trustworthy if someone with a practising certificate has read it and agreed it states the law correctly. That’s the job.

What you are checking

The law itself, written out as rules a machine can run: cgt_a1.le. It takes a CGT event A1 disposal (contract date, acquisition date, proceeds, cost-base elements, entity kind, asset classification) and gives one of three answers: a capital gain or loss and the net capital gain for the income year; “cannot be assessed because <fact> is missing”; or “refused because <provision> not encoded”. 114 rules, 62 fictional scenarios, 163 embedded tests, every expected figure worked by hand from the Act (shown above each scenario). No software to run: read the rules against the Act.

The provisions in scope are ITAA 1997 Parts 3-1 and 3-3: ss 102-5, 102-10, 102-15, 104-10, 108-10, 108-17, 108-20, 108-30, 110-25, 110-45, 110-55, 112-20, 116-20, 116-30, 118-5, 118-10, 118-20, 118-24, 118-25, 118-30, 118-37, 118-60, 118-300, Div 115; and IT(TP)A 1997 s 102-25.

What we most want a practitioner’s view on

  1. Loss order (Q2). s 102-5 step 1 note 1 and step 2 note 2 leave the order of applying losses to the taxpayer. The file applies losses to non-discount gains first (scenario s7: 9,000, versus 11,000 the other way). Is encoding the minimising default acceptable, or must the order be an input?
  2. Break-it. Construct facts within scope where the file returns a plausible figure that is wrong. Scenarios s11 (PUA at exactly $10,000), s13 (cost base vs reduced cost base gap, s 110-45(2)), s52 (leap-year anniversary, TD 2002/10), s54 (loss-year strictness) are where we looked.
  3. The 17 numbered questions in open_questions.md (answer inline).
  4. Enter scenario s7 in the ATO CGT record keeping tool and record its figure (we could not complete this by automation — our attempt and the expected points of difference are summarised in the brief below).

The question we value most

s 102-5 step 1 note 1 and step 2 note 2 leave the order of applying losses to the taxpayer. The file applies losses to non-discount gains first (scenario s7: 9,000, versus 11,000 the other way). Is encoding the minimising default acceptable, or must the order be an input?

This is the judgement the statute leaves open. It is worth the fee on its own. Here is scenario s7 as it stands in the file (quoted; please do not edit the file — its SHA-256 is part of the evidence chain): 

s7 ordering: d1 gain 20000 (discount: held since 2020); d2 gain 4000
(acquired 2024-09-01, sold 2025-01-15: not 12 months); d3 loss 6000.
Step 1 (s 102-5): the taxpayer chooses the order; the file applies the
loss to the non-discount gain first: 4000 -> 0, remaining 2000 against
20000 -> 18000. Step 3: 18000 x 50% = 9000. Net capital gain 9000.
(Applying the loss to the discount gain first would give
14000 x 50% + 4000 = 11000: the choice matters.)

Out of scope

The file refuses these with a reason: main residence, Div 152, roll-overs, deceased estates, foreign residents at the event, indexation, events other than A1, K7, Subdiv 115-C, life insurance companies, pre-9 May 2012 foreign residency periods, substituted accounting periods, leases granted.
