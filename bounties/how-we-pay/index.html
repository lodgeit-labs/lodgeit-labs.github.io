The programme

How we pay reviewers today

See also How we work, How we get quality, From bounties to ownership.

Every bounty on this site pays for the same three things, kept separate because they are different acts.

1. The review: a flat fee, whatever you find

You are paid the bounty's stated fee for a completed review. A review that finds nothing earns the same as one that finds three defects. A REJECT verdict earns the same as an ACCEPT. We pay for the hours and the attention; the verdict is yours.

A completed review means the verdict form filled in for your part of the file, with rule references picked from the file itself and the scenarios you traced named. On rule-file bounties that also means at least two fact patterns run through the file ("try it") so we can see the review engaged with the rules, not just the prose.

2. The finding: a graded bonus, paid on acceptance

A finding is a specific defect: this rule, this provision, this is wrong, this is what the Act requires. Accepted findings earn a bonus on top of the fee, graded by what they would have cost a user:

GradeWhat it isTypical bonus
1A plausible wrong figure on an in-scope fact patternhighest
2A fact the Act requires that the file silently defaulted
3A case the file should refuse but answers
4A test, comment or documentation defectlowest

The bounty page states the bonus per grade. A finding is accepted when we reproduce it on the file's published hash; it then goes into the findings log with your name (or initials, or "verified practitioner", as you choose) and the fix rolls the file to a new version. Findings we cannot reproduce are returned to you with the reason; they are not published and they do not count against you beyond our own records.

Break-it bounties are findings bounties with no review attached: construct a fact pattern within scope where the file returns a plausible wrong figure, and be paid per accepted case.

3. The signature: a separate question, and a separate fee

At the end of every rule-file review is a question that is not part of the verdict: do you sign this exact file, identified by its hash, as stating the law for the scope in its header? Yes, no, or yes once named findings are fixed. You are paid for the review whatever you answer. "No" needs no reason.

Because a signature covers the whole file, family-sized reviews usually end with "no" and the signatures come from a short sign-off bounty after the fixes are in: one more read of the whole revised file, one hour, its own fee. Two signatures from different verified practitioners, at least one of them currently licensed, make a file Ratified. One makes it Reviewed. None makes it what it is: unreviewed, and labelled so wherever it runs.

A signature binds to the hash. When the file changes, the signature lapses and the new file starts at zero. Your name appears in the public registry against the hash you signed, never against any individual answer the file gives to anyone.

What a bounty page tells you

Fee for the review; bonus per finding grade; whether there is a sign-off round and its fee; expected hours; who may claim it (registered agents, legal practitioners, CA/CPA/IPA members, former practitioners); the file's hash and reasoner version; and the deadline. Prices move: if a bounty sits unclaimed we raise it, and we say so on the page.

Getting paid

On acceptance, the portal (or until it exists, our support team) sends you a payment reference and the amounts, ex GST and with GST if you are registered. You invoice us against the reference; we run payouts monthly. Named credit on the published review and in the registry is the default; you can choose initials or "verified practitioner" instead.
