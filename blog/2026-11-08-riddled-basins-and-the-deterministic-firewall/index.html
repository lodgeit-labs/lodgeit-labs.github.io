Riddled basins — why you cannot prompt-engineer your way to deterministic accounting

A landmark physics paper published this week in Nature Machine Intelligence proves that neural network unpredictability is rooted in fractal basin geometry. Here is why ten orders of magnitude of extra precision buys zero reproducibility — and why LodgeiT Labs isolates probabilistic AI behind SBRM and Logical English.

The “next model will fix it” fallacy

Whenever we walk practitioners or enterprise engineers through the LodgeiT Labs stack, the same objection eventually surfaces. It usually sounds like this:

“We understand why you built SWI-Prolog calculators and Logical English rules today. LLMs hallucinate denominators and invert statutory anti-avoidance tests. But frontier models are getting better every six months. Once we have deterministic hardware kernels, temperature = 0, pinned seeds, and models fine-tuned directly on the tax legislation, won’t the neural network just compute the return directly?”

It is a tempting story. It implies that AI unpredictability is merely an engineering blemish — a byproduct of stochastic data shuffling, floating-point hardware noise, or insufficient training scale. If you just tighten the tolerances, the thinking goes, a neural network will eventually behave like a calculator.

This week, two physicists at the University of Sydney proved mathematically that this story is false.

In a paper published in Nature Machine Intelligence (“Fractal basin geometry and the limits to predictability and reproducibility in deep learning”, DOI: 10.1038/s42256-026-01336-3), Ph.D. researcher Andrew Ly and Professor Pulin Gong from the University of Sydney’s School of Physics demonstrated that the unpredictability of deep learning is not an engineering flaw or a hardware quirk. It is a fundamental mathematical property of how gradient-based neural networks learn.

You cannot engineer your way out of it by increasing precision. You can only architect around it.

A basin with no inside

To understand what Ly and Gong discovered, consider how a neural network learns. During training, an optimization algorithm navigates a massive, high-dimensional landscape of millions or billions of parameters, adjusting weights step by step to minimize error.

Mathematicians think of this landscape as a map divided into “basins of attraction.” Every possible starting setup for a model is a coordinate on that map, colored according to the final behavior (or internal solution) the network eventually settles into. If Blue represents Outcome A and Orange represents Outcome B, an ordinary, well-behaved basin has an inside. Close to the boundary ridge between Blue and Orange, a tiny nudge might tip your model from one outcome to the other. But deep inside the Blue basin, a whole neighborhood of points leads reliably to the exact same destination. If you increase your numerical precision and shrink your uncertainty, you stand clear of the border.

Ly and Gong trained roughly 500,000 neural networks across image recognition and text processing architectures in fully deterministic computing environments — stripping away external randomness like data-presentation order and hardware variation.

What they found is that neural network basins do not have a safe “inside.” Instead, they are organized as intricate fractals known in dynamical systems theory as riddled basins.

The geometry of a riddled basin. In a riddled landscape, the basin is inseparable from its own border. Every single point leading to Outcome A lies arbitrarily close to points — of positive volume — that lead to Outcome B. No matter how many times you zoom in, Blue is shot through with Orange at every scale, all the way down.

The empirical numbers from the study should stop every financial software architect cold:

  • Ten billion times more precision bought nothing. In one larger network experiment, increasing the numerical precision of the starting conditions by ten orders of magnitude ($10^{10}$) produced zero measurable improvement in reproducibility.
  • A single-bit flip. Altering a network’s initial configuration by the smallest quantity a computer can register — a single least-significant bit — sent the model to a completely different internal configuration in nearly half (about 48%) of all cases.
  • The capability-reproducibility trap. The researchers discovered a structural trade-off between performance and reproducibility. The exact hyperparameter settings (such as higher learning rates) that enable neural networks to learn complex patterns effectively are the same settings that drive the system into chaotic, riddled fractal regimes. When the team reduced the learning rate to force reproducibility, the basins smoothed out — but the networks lost their ability to learn effectively.

In short: the mathematical dynamics that make a neural network smart are the exact dynamics that make its internal state space fractal and non-reproducible.

Why a riddled manifold cannot hold a ledger

In consumer applications, a riddled basin is a curiosity. If two identically trained language models converge on different internal weight configurations and phrase an email slightly differently, nobody gets audited.

In statutory accounting and tax compliance, a riddled basin is fatal.

A trial balance is a zero-sum algebraic identity: debits (positive) and credits (negative) either sum to zero, or the books are out of balance. Under Division 7A of the ITAA 1936, a $\$100,000$ amalgamated loan either meets its s 109E minimum yearly repayment by the lodgement day, or a deemed unfranked dividend crystallises under s 109D. Under Parts 3-1 and 3-3 of the ITAA 1997, a CGT Event A1 disposal either satisfies the conditions for a discount and small-business concession, or it does not.

Statutory law and double-entry bookkeeping do not exist on a continuous probability manifold. They exist in a discrete state space where boundaries are razor-sharp and outcomes are binary or exact to the cent.

When you ask a pure neural network to act as both the reader of messy reality and the executor of statutory logic, you are running compliance inside a riddled basin. Because the model’s internal representation is interwoven at every scale, an imperceptible perturbation in context — an extra line item in a trial balance, a slightly reworded clause in a trust deed, a minor model checkpoint update — can shift the trajectory across a fractal boundary into an entirely different computational basin.

That is the mathematical root of the four failure modes we documented in our Division 7A benchmark: boolean routing collapse, look-through state mutation, advisory inversion on s 109R, and denominator hallucination. Those weren’t prompt bugs. They were trajectories jumping across a riddled basin.

1. Embracing the fractal where we need it (the Proposer)

Recall Ly and Gong’s trade-off: if you force a neural network to be strictly reproducible by starving its learning rate, it stops learning effectively.

We want high-capability neural networks in our outer ring. Reading an unstructured 40-page commercial lease, parsing a messy PDF invoice, or classifying raw bank feed narratives requires navigating high-dimensional ambiguity. In our stack — across ClawDog, TaxGenii, and the Fano Classifier — we let the neural network operate in its high-learning-rate regime where it excels: proposing structure from chaos.

2. SBRM as the phase-transition firewall (discretization)

How do you stop the fractal instability of the neural network from infecting the general ledger or the tax return? You force every neural proposal through a rigid, low-dimensional algebraic funnel before any calculation occurs.

In our platform, that funnel is SBRM (Charles Hoffman’s Standard Business Reporting Model) paired with our Semantic Firewall and Operator-Approval Boundary.

  • A neural model is never allowed to emit a final tax liability or post directly to a ledger.
  • Instead, its continuous vector state is forced to collapse into discrete, typed semantic propositions: Entity $X$, CGT Event A1, Contract Date $T_1$, Capital Proceeds $P$, Cost Base Elements $\{C_1, C_2\}$, polarity-checked against a seven-way accounting taxonomy.
  • Behind an asynchronous staging boundary, the human practitioner inspects the dry-run diff and the source witness. Once ratified, the fuzzy, riddled state space of the neural net is dead. What survives is an immutable, hash-bound YAML/Markdown fact contract.

3. Logical English as a fractal-free zone (the Disposer)

Once those discrete facts cross the firewall into Logical English (.le) and compile down to SWI-Prolog, gradient descent does not exist. There are no loss landscapes, no learning rates, and no riddled basins.

When our CGT Event A1 engine (114 rules, 62 worked scenarios, 163 test cases) or our Division 7A engine executes against a ratified fact graph, it performs logical unification and resolution over discrete predicates.

  • Given the same fact contract, the engine will resolve the exact same statutory liability and emit the exact same proof tree 100% of the time — today, tomorrow, or when an ATO or HMRC auditor replays the hash receipt three years from now.
  • And when that fact graph is anchored externally via Proof-of-Control (remote signing over Nostr / NIP-46) and settled via The Scene Protocol, both the input facts and the logical derivation are cryptographically locked.

Replacing hope with math

At the end of their paper, Professor Gong summarizes the challenge facing artificial intelligence across society:

“To build trustworthy systems, we need not only accurate predictions but also confidence that those predictions can be reproduced. This research provides a new way of understanding the limits of predictability in AI, and how we might overcome them.”

In accounting, tax, and financial reporting, “hoping the model behaves consistently” was never an acceptable engineering posture. Now we know it is a mathematical impossibility. You cannot prompt-engineer, fine-tune, or scale your way out of a riddled fractal basin.

The only way to build trustworthy financial AI is to respect the mathematics of both worlds: use probabilistic models to propose structure from messy human activity, collapse their outputs across a hard algebraic boundary (SBRM), and execute the law in a deterministic symbolic substrate (Logical English) where every rule is ratified by a named practitioner and every answer carries a proof.

That is why we don’t bolt an LLM onto a general ledger. That is why the methodology is the moat.
