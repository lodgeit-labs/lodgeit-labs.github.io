Rounding

One rule for every calculator in the constellation, so the same facts give the same cents wherever they are computed.
LodgeiT Labs · November 2026 · policy lodgeit-rounding-1.0

Why a page about cents

A single calculation that is a cent out is a curiosity. A million calculations that are each a cent out, in the same direction, is a ledger that does not reconcile, a return that does not match its working papers, and an agent that cannot tell a defect from a convention. Rounding is where otherwise-correct engines most often disagree, and the disagreement is almost never about the arithmetic; it is about where and how often a figure was rounded.

So the constellation has one standard, every response names it, and any change to it is a new, dated policy, never a silent one.

The standard: lodgeit-rounding-1.0

Where a statute or an authority's form prescribes its own rounding, that rule prevails. This standard governs everything else.

  1. Exact inside. Arithmetic is exact (rational or arbitrary-precision decimal). No binary floating point between the parsed input and the emitted figure.
  2. One rounding point. A figure is rounded once, when it is emitted. A balance published at the end of an income year or other reporting period is an emission: that figure, in cents, opens the next period. Otherwise a rounded value is never fed back into arithmetic, except as rule 6 allows.
  3. Cents, ties away from zero. Money emits at 2 dp, ties away from zero (ROUND_HALF_UP, MidpointRounding.AwayFromZero, Excel ROUND). Rates and factors emit at 4 dp on the same rule. Rate inputs are accepted at the precision given. An emitted 4 dp rate is for display: never feed it back into arithmetic or pass it to another calculator.
  4. Cent-quantised in, refused otherwise. Money inputs must already be cents. A finer value is refused with 422 money_not_cent_quantised naming the field, never silently rounded.
  5. Published totals foot. Where lines and a total are published together, the total is the sum of the published lines, and any difference from the exact total is shown as a working (for example totals.rounding_residual).
  6. The posted unit is the rounded line. When one rounded figure is apportioned into posted periods, each period is rounded and the last carries the difference. Each posted period is an emission in the sense of rule 2.
  7. Schedules never absorb. A schedule is not forced to close at zero. A non-zero end balance is a fact about the contract or the rate, and is reported as such. Where a statute itself closes a schedule, that rule applies.
  8. Whole dollars only at the label. Whole dollars appear only where an authority's form label requires them (for Australian returns, the ATO label), once, by one named function applying that label's stated direction. Never by a cast, and never inside a running balance.

Every 200 names the policy in force in manifest.rounding_policy, and its advisory says which of rules 3, 5, 6 and 7 apply.

Three consequences people ask about

Balances carry cents, not dollars

A pool balance, a written-down value or a loan balance is a running ledger, not a form label. It carries cents from year to year. Rounding it to whole dollars each year shifts the balance by up to fifty cents a year. The effect is bounded, not compounding: at a 37.5% pool rate it stays within about $1.33. Some forms and worksheets do carry whole dollars, including the ATO's own low-value pool worksheet. The constellation carries cents and records the difference. Whole dollars are produced when the figure is placed on a return label, and only there (rule 8).

For Australian returns the direction is set by law: where an approved form asks for whole dollars, the amount is truncated (Taxation Administration Act 1953, Schedule 1, section 388-85). The exact total is 2,097.20, which goes on the label as 2,097. Rounding each asset first gives 2,098, a dollar over, and the error grows with the number of lines. Some labels take cents, so the direction is set label by label. The constellation emits each asset in cents and leaves the single whole-dollar step to whoever assembles the return.

Why ties away from zero, not to even

Rounding half to even removes a small upward bias when many rounded values are added together. That bias is small here: figures are computed exactly and rounded once, and where published lines are added (rule 5) any difference from the exact total is shown, not hidden. Ties away from zero matches what practitioners, spreadsheets (ROUND) and most published calculators do. The choice between the two moves at most a cent on an exact tie; rounding more than once moves dollars.

Days, years and leap years

Where a statute apportions by time, the calculator uses the statute's formula, not a calendar library's. For Australian decline in value that is days held ÷ 365, where days held can be 366 in a leap year (ATO formula). So a full income year containing 29 February gives 366/365 of the annual amount. That is the law, not a defect, and a calculator using 366/366 is the one that disagrees with it. Each calculator states its day-count rule in its description. This applies to tax decline in value. Accounting depreciation follows the day-count convention named in the request (actual/actual by default).

Versioning: how the rule changes

  • Never silently. A change in rounding behaviour is a new policy identifier (for example lodgeit-rounding-1.1), published here with its first effective period.
  • On a period boundary. A new policy applies from a stated income year. A result computed for an earlier period keeps the policy it was computed under.
  • Always named. Because every response carries its policy and its rate-table hashes, any historical result can be reproduced, and any difference between two results can be traced to the rule that caused it.
  • Jurisdictions may differ. Where an authority mandates a different direction for a label or a line, that is a named policy for that jurisdiction, not a local exception inside one calculator.

The constellation figure follows the published standard; a difference explained by this table is one to record, not a defect in the constellation figure. If it is not explained by this table, please report it: the request, the response, the figure you expected and where that expectation comes from.

For calculator authors

  • Use a decimal or rational type end to end, including any hop between your own services.
  • Keep one rounding function per scale (cents, 4 dp, whole-dollar label) and call it only at emission.
  • Emit manifest.rounding_policy on every 200.
  • Put an exact half-cent tie, a multi-line total and (if you apportion by days) a leap-year case in your fixture.
